# Chicago Blackhawks 2025–26: Offensive vs. Defensive Performance

## Project Overview

For this project, I analyzed the Chicago Blackhawks' performance during the 2025–26 NHL regular season to better understand why the team struggled to win games. I focused on whether the team's performance was more affected by its offense or its defense.

I compared Chicago's goals scored and goals allowed with NHL averages and examined how the team's performance differed between wins and losses. I used Excel to organize the data, calculate performance metrics, and create visualizations to communicate the results.

## Business Question

**Were the Chicago Blackhawks losing games because of poor offense or poor defense?**

## Tools Used

- Microsoft Excel
- Data cleaning and organization
- Formulas and calculated performance metrics
- Data visualization and dashboard creation

## Key Findings

- **Below-average scoring:** Chicago averaged approximately 2.56 goals per game, compared with the NHL average of approximately 3.08.
- **Defensive struggles:** Chicago allowed approximately 3.29 goals per game, compared with the NHL average of approximately 3.08.
- **Challenges in losses:** In regulation losses, Chicago averaged approximately 1.59 goals scored and 4.46 goals allowed per game.
- **Stronger performance in wins:** In games Chicago won, the team averaged approximately 3.90 goals scored and 1.79 goals allowed per game.

## Conclusion

The analysis suggests that both offensive and defensive performance contributed to Chicago's struggles during the 2025–26 season. The team scored less frequently than the NHL average and allowed more goals than the average team.

Offense was a clear weakness, but the team's defensive performance was also a major issue, particularly in regulation losses. Based on these results, I would not attribute the team's struggles to one area alone. Additional analysis of shots on goal, special teams, and goaltending performance could help identify the factors most closely associated with winning and losing.

## What I Learned

This project gave me hands-on experience organizing game-level data, calculating performance metrics, comparing results against league benchmarks, and building visualizations to communicate findings. It also helped me practice turning a broad question into a data analysis and drawing conclusions while recognizing the limitations of the available data.
